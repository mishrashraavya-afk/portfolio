Beyond the Face

A proposed project on EEG-based approaches to rehabilitation. It is not yet a completed piece of work, and this page does not claim results.

Can EEG-derived information offer a useful complement to facial expression in rehabilitation research connected to Moebius syndrome?

Status
Proposed. No outcome, accuracy figure or participant data exists to report.
Motivation
Facial expression alone may not communicate every emotional response. This research concept asks whether EEG-derived information could provide a complementary signal for rehabilitation research, with the people affected and their needs at the centre.
Related prior work
CalmO, a brainwave-controlled music synthesizer (neurotechnology interface, signal processing, human-computer interaction). See More projects.
Proposed methods
Planned pipeline: identify an appropriate, documented EEG dataset; define preprocessing and artifact handling; extract features; compare candidate models using a held-out validation design. These steps remain proposed until documented in the project record.
Limitations
EEG signals are noisy and emotion-related labels are imperfect. A model prediction is not a direct reading of subjective experience. Results from a general dataset cannot establish validity for people with Moebius syndrome or improve rehabilitation outcomes.
Validation needed
Any future claim would require transparent dataset provenance, appropriate held-out evaluation, uncertainty and failure analysis, participant-informed measures, ethical review and population-specific validation with relevant clinical oversight.
My contribution
I developed the research concept and proposed methodology, including the use of EEG-based analysis to investigate neurological patterns beyond externally observable facial characteristics. I conducted a preliminary literature review and helped define the research question, potential experimental framework and analytical approach. The project is presented as a proposed research concept, not a completed experimental study.

SOLACE

Solubility Optimization through Learning, Analysis and Computational Estimation: machine-learning prediction of aqueous solubility from molecular descriptors. Manuscript submitted to Cureus (AI-augmented research); not yet peer reviewed or published.

Status
Manuscript submitted to Cureus for AI-augmented research. Not yet peer reviewed or published.
Research question
Can machine-learning models trained on molecular descriptors accurately predict the aqueous solubility of chemical compounds, and provide an accessible computational tool for early-stage solubility estimation?
Related prior work
ProtMut, a molecular dynamics simulator for mutant protein structure and function (Python, OpenMM, Flask, AlphaFold-2), mentored by Prof. Sudipta Tung. See More projects.
Methods
Curate and clean aqueous solubility data from AqSolDB, generate molecular descriptors with RDKit, and train and compare regression models (Linear Regression, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting) on held-out test data, using R², RMSE, MAE and correlation metrics. The best-performing model is integrated into a user-friendly prediction workflow.
Limitations
The model is limited by the quality, size and chemical diversity of the available dataset. Experimental conditions across reported solubility measurements vary, which introduces noise. Predictions based on a limited set of molecular descriptors may not capture all factors that influence real-world solubility.
Validation needed
Validation on independent external datasets and experimentally measured compounds is needed to assess generalisability. Comparing predictions with laboratory-generated solubility measurements would give stronger evidence of real-world performance.
My contribution
I developed the computational workflow, including dataset curation and preprocessing, descriptor generation with RDKit, machine-learning model development and optimisation, performance evaluation, and interpretation of results. I also contributed to developing SOLACE as an accessible framework for predicting aqueous solubility.

I-SRAVIA

Indian Sign Language Responsive And Voice Intelligent Assistant. Two-way communication between ISL and text or voice, with speech-to-text in real time.

Status
Ongoing since 2022. Founder-led venture.
Method
Computer vision and machine learning. Stack: Python, OpenCV, TensorFlow, MediaPipe, Flask.

Limitations

HemoMaap

Hemo (blood) and Maap (measurement, size, standard). A complete blood count analyser and sickle cell detection tool.

Status
Ongoing since 2025.
Mentor
Prof. Hari Shroff, HHMI Janelia.
Method
Computer-vision analysis of medical images. Stack: Cellpose, Flask.
Limitations
The approach is constrained by the size and diversity of the available blood-smear image dataset. Image quality, staining differences, microscope settings, and overlapping or irregularly shaped cells may affect segmentation and classification. It is a screening and research-support tool and needs further clinical validation before any diagnostic use.
Validation needed
The model needs evaluation on larger, independent datasets with images from different laboratories and imaging conditions. Validation against expert annotations and clinically confirmed samples is needed to assess robustness, sensitivity and real-world generalisability.
My contribution
I designed and developed the computational pipeline, including image preprocessing, cell segmentation using computer-vision techniques, individual cell extraction, and a machine-learning workflow to distinguish normal from sickle-shaped cells. I also worked on turning the pipeline into a prototype application that estimates the proportion of affected cells.

SanGo

A mental health app by students, for students. A chatbot with sentiment analysis, built for Rashtriya Bal Vigyanik Pradarshini.

Method. A student-focused conversational support system using natural-language processing to give supportive, non-clinical responses and direct users to trusted support when needed.

Limitations. A chatbot cannot understand emotions or circumstances as deeply as a qualified human professional and is not a replacement for professional or trusted human support.

ADIONA

Drone hardware design and prototype with computer-vision detection. External GPU resources secured through independent institutional outreach. Built for the World Robotics Olympiad.

Method. A drone captures images of an industrial environment and a computer-vision model flags potential injuries or emergencies for human attention. It is an assistive monitoring tool, not an autonomous decision-maker.

Limitations. Detection can be affected by lighting, angle, distance, occlusion and weather, may give false positives or misses, and cannot judge the severity or medical nature of an injury.

Validation needed. Testing on realistic industrial scenarios with accuracy and false-positive/negative rates, plus safety, privacy and regulatory evaluation before any deployment.

Listed

CalmO

A brainwave-controlled music synthesizer: a neurotechnology interface for music generation, exploring signal processing and human-computer interaction.

Limitations. Effectiveness may vary substantially between individuals, because physiological and emotional responses to music are highly personal. Sensor noise, limited training data, and the difficulty of inferring emotional state from multimodal inputs may affect performance.

Validation needed. Controlled user studies comparing adaptive and non-adaptive music experiences, with physiological signals, voluntary feedback and standardised measures of user experience, in a larger and more diverse group.
